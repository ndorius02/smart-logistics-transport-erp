# Inventory Management — Business Rules

## 1. Purpose

The **Inventory Management** module manages the stock state of products inside warehouses.

Inventory represents the relationship between:

- one Product;
- one Warehouse;
- the stock quantities associated with that Product in that Warehouse.

Inventory Management does not define product master data.

Product information such as SKU, name, category, unit of measure and weight belongs to Product Management.

Inventory Management answers the business question:

```text
Where is this product stored, and how much stock is available?
```

---

## 2. Inventory Definition

An Inventory record represents the stock state of one Product in one Warehouse.

An inventory record contains:

- Technical identifier
- Product
- Warehouse
- Physical quantity
- Reserved quantity
- Minimum stock level
- Creation timestamp
- Last update timestamp

Example:

```text
Product:
PRD-ELEC-001
Dell Latitude 5550

Warehouse:
WH-BRU-001
Brussels Distribution Center

Quantity:
120

Reserved Quantity:
20

Available Quantity:
100

Minimum Stock Level:
30
```

---

## 3. Relationship Between Product and Warehouse

The relationship is:

```text
Product
   1
   |
   |
   *
Inventory
   *
   |
   |
   1
Warehouse
```

A Product may exist in many Warehouses.

A Warehouse may contain many Products.

Inventory acts as the association between Product and Warehouse.

---

## 4. Business Rules

### BR-INV-001 — Inventory Identifier

Each Inventory record must have a unique technical identifier.

The identifier is generated by the application.

API clients must not provide or modify it.

---

### BR-INV-002 — Product Is Required

Every Inventory record must reference exactly one Product.

The Product must exist in the database.

---

### BR-INV-003 — Warehouse Is Required

Every Inventory record must reference exactly one Warehouse.

The Warehouse must exist in the database.

---

### BR-INV-004 — Product and Warehouse Combination Must Be Unique

A Product may have only one Inventory record per Warehouse.

The combination:

```text
product_id + warehouse_id
```

must therefore be unique.

Example:

Valid:

```text
PRD-ELEC-001 + WH-BRU-001
PRD-ELEC-001 + WH-ANT-001
```

Invalid:

```text
PRD-ELEC-001 + WH-BRU-001
PRD-ELEC-001 + WH-BRU-001
```

The database must enforce:

```text
UNIQUE(product_id, warehouse_id)
```

---

### BR-INV-005 — Physical Quantity

`quantity` represents the current physical quantity of the Product stored in the Warehouse.

The quantity:

- is mandatory;
- must never be negative;
- starts at zero when a new inventory position is created.

Example:

```text
quantity = 0
```

is valid.

```text
quantity = -5
```

is invalid.

---

### BR-INV-006 — Reserved Quantity

`reservedQuantity` represents stock that physically exists but is already reserved for future operations.

Examples of future reservations may include:

- shipment preparation;
- cargo allocation;
- customer delivery;
- internal logistics operations.

The reserved quantity:

- is mandatory;
- starts at zero;
- must never be negative;
- must never exceed the physical quantity.

Rule:

```text
reservedQuantity <= quantity
```

---

### BR-INV-007 — Available Quantity

Available quantity represents stock that is physically present and not reserved.

It is calculated as:

```text
availableQuantity =
    quantity - reservedQuantity
```

Example:

```text
quantity = 100
reservedQuantity = 25

availableQuantity = 75
```

`availableQuantity` must not be stored independently in the database.

It is a derived business value.

This avoids synchronization problems between:

```text
quantity
reservedQuantity
availableQuantity
```

---

### BR-INV-008 — Inventory Must Never Become Negative

An Inventory operation must never produce:

```text
quantity < 0
```

or:

```text
reservedQuantity < 0
```

The backend must reject any operation that would violate these conditions.

---

### BR-INV-009 — Reserved Quantity Must Not Exceed Physical Stock

The following condition must always remain true:

```text
reservedQuantity <= quantity
```

Example:

Valid:

```text
quantity = 50
reservedQuantity = 20
```

Invalid:

```text
quantity = 50
reservedQuantity = 60
```

---

### BR-INV-010 — Minimum Stock Level

Each Inventory position may define a minimum stock level.

The minimum stock level:

- is mandatory in V1;
- must be zero or greater;
- represents the operational threshold below which stock should be considered low.

Example:

```text
minimumStockLevel = 20
```

---

### BR-INV-011 — Low Stock

An Inventory position is considered low stock when:

```text
availableQuantity <= minimumStockLevel
```

Example:

```text
quantity = 25
reservedQuantity = 10
availableQuantity = 15
minimumStockLevel = 20
```

Result:

```text
LOW STOCK
```

Low-stock status is derived and must not be stored as a separate database column.

---

### BR-INV-012 — Product Must Be Active for New Inventory Positions

A new Inventory position may only be created for an active Product.

If:

```text
Product.active = false
```

the backend must reject creation of a new Inventory position.

Existing inventory records remain readable if the Product is later deactivated.

---

### BR-INV-013 — Warehouse Must Be Active for New Inventory Positions

A new Inventory position may only be created for an active Warehouse.

If:

```text
Warehouse.active = false
```

the backend must reject creation of a new Inventory position.

Existing inventory records remain available for historical purposes if the Warehouse is later deactivated.

---

### BR-INV-014 — Inventory Creation Does Not Represent Stock Receipt

Creating an Inventory record creates the stock position only.

It must start with:

```text
quantity = 0
reservedQuantity = 0
```

Creating Inventory must not automatically create physical stock.

Physical stock will later be changed using Stock Movement operations.

---

### BR-INV-015 — Direct Quantity Modification Is Restricted

Normal business operations must not directly overwrite physical stock quantities.

For example, the following should not become the normal business API:

```text
PUT quantity = 500
```

Stock quantity changes must eventually be performed through Stock Movement operations.

This ensures traceability.

---

### BR-INV-016 — Inventory Configuration Update

Authorized users may update configuration fields such as:

```text
minimumStockLevel
```

However, configuration updates must not directly modify:

```text
quantity
reservedQuantity
```

Those values belong to operational stock processes.

---

### BR-INV-017 — No Physical Delete

Inventory records must not normally be physically deleted once stock history exists.

Historical references must remain consistent.

For V1, no public DELETE endpoint will be provided.

---

### BR-INV-018 — Audit Information

The application must maintain:

```text
createdAt
updatedAt
```

These values are managed by the existing `Auditable` base entity.

API clients must not provide or modify them.

---

## 5. Inventory Initial State

When a Product is first assigned to a Warehouse:

```text
quantity = 0
reservedQuantity = 0
minimumStockLevel = configured value
```

Example:

```text
Product:
PRD-ELEC-001

Warehouse:
WH-BRU-001

quantity:
0

reservedQuantity:
0

minimumStockLevel:
10
```

Stock is added later through Stock Movement operations.

---

## 6. Inventory Calculated Values

### Available Quantity

```text
availableQuantity =
    quantity - reservedQuantity
```

### Low Stock

```text
lowStock =
    availableQuantity <= minimumStockLevel
```

These values are calculated by the application.

They are not stored independently.

---

## 7. Search and Retrieval Rules

Users must be able to retrieve Inventory:

- by Warehouse;
- by Product;
- by Product and Warehouse;
- by low-stock status.

---

### Inventory by Warehouse

Example:

```http
GET /api/inventory/warehouse/{warehouseId}
```

This answers:

```text
What products are stored in this warehouse?
```

---

### Inventory by Product

Example:

```http
GET /api/inventory/product/{productId}
```

This answers:

```text
In which warehouses is this product stored?
```

---

### Specific Inventory Position

Example:

```http
GET /api/inventory/product/{productId}/warehouse/{warehouseId}
```

This returns the unique stock position for the Product/Warehouse combination.

---

### Low Stock

Example:

```http
GET /api/inventory/low-stock
```

This returns Inventory records where:

```text
availableQuantity <= minimumStockLevel
```

---

## 8. Pagination

Inventory list endpoints must support pagination where appropriate.

Supported parameters:

```text
page
size
sort
```

Example:

```http
GET /api/inventory?page=0&size=10&sort=createdAt,desc
```

## 12. Database Constraints

The database must protect essential Inventory invariants.

Required constraints include:

```text
PRIMARY KEY (id)
```

```text
FOREIGN KEY (product_id)
REFERENCES products(id)
```

```text
FOREIGN KEY (warehouse_id)
REFERENCES warehouse(id)
```

```text
UNIQUE(product_id, warehouse_id)
```

```text
CHECK(quantity >= 0)
```

```text
CHECK(reserved_quantity >= 0)
```

```text
CHECK(reserved_quantity <= quantity)
```

```text
CHECK(minimum_stock_level >= 0)
```

## 13. Inventory vs Stock Movement

Inventory represents:

```text
CURRENT STOCK STATE
```

StockMovement represents:

```text
WHY AND HOW THE STOCK CHANGED
```

Example:

```text
Inventory:
quantity = 100
```

After receiving:

```text
StockMovement:
STOCK_IN +20
```

Inventory becomes:

```text
quantity = 120
```

Therefore:

```text
Inventory
→ current state

StockMovement
→ transaction/history
```

Both concepts must remain separate.

---

## 14. Warehouse Stock Tracking

Warehouse stock tracking will use Inventory data to provide warehouse-centric views.

Example:

```text
Warehouse WH-BRU-001

Product                Qty   Reserved   Available
--------------------------------------------------
Dell Latitude          120      20        100
Safety Helmet          250       0        250
Medical Gloves         500      50        450
```

Warehouse stock tracking is therefore primarily a query/view capability built on Inventory.

## 15. Scope of Inventory Management V1

### Included

- Inventory position creation
- Product/Warehouse relationship
- Unique stock position per Product/Warehouse
- Physical quantity
- Reserved quantity
- Available quantity calculation
- Minimum stock level
- Low-stock detection
- Search by Warehouse
- Search by Product
- Product/Warehouse lookup
- Pagination
- Audit timestamps
- Role-based authorization

### Not Included

- Physical stock increase/decrease API
- Stock reservations
- Reservation release
- Warehouse transfers
- Batch tracking
- Serial-number tracking
- Expiration tracking
- Purchase-order receiving
- Shipment allocation
- Inventory valuation

These operational changes will be introduced through Stock Movement and future modules.

---

## 16. Summary

Inventory is the **current warehouse stock state** for a specific Product and Warehouse combination.

Its responsibilities are:

- connecting Products to Warehouses;
- maintaining physical quantities;
- maintaining reserved quantities;
- calculating available quantities;
- managing minimum stock thresholds;
- detecting low stock;
- providing Product-based and Warehouse-based stock visibility.

Inventory does not describe the Product itself.

Inventory also does not explain the history of stock changes.

Those responsibilities belong respectively to:

```text
Product Management
and
Stock Movement Management
```
